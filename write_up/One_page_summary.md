# The Blacklist Gap - One-Page Summary

**Question:** When one Indian government drug buyer bans a supplier for bad
quality, do other buyers keep it eligible? India has no single national
blacklist for drug suppliers - 9 separate buyers' public records were
checked by hand against each other for this review.

Buyers may have reasons not visible in public records; the firms and
buyers were not contacted for comment. Every finding below is a **possible
breach of a tender clause as worded**, not a finding of fraud or illegal
conduct.

## The 3 headline cases

| Firm | Banned by | For | Active on Rajasthan's bid deadline? | Estimated contract value (2-year, not amount paid) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zee Laboratories Ltd | Madhya Pradesh (MPPHSCL), 3 injectables, statutory lab (CDL Kolkata) | 2024 NSQ findings | Yes, on all 3 of its Rajasthan deadlines | Rs 25-46 crore |
| Meril Endo Surgery Pvt Ltd | Gujarat (GMSCL), surgical suture, statutory lab (Food & Drug Laboratory, Vadodara) | 2025 NSQ finding (sterility) | Yes | Rs 1-2.3 crore |
| Nestor Pharmaceuticals Ltd | Andhra Pradesh (APMSIDC), Iron & Folic Acid Syrup, statutory lab (DCL Vijayawada) | 2023 NSQ finding | Yes | Rs 3-5.3 crore |

## Key numbers

- **804** ban records across 9 buyers; **371** are quality findings.
- **63 firms** are actively banned by 2 or more buyers at once.
- **37 rows** are a formal, statutory "Not of Standard Quality" finding
  (Q1); **334** are a quality finding without a named statutory lab (Q2).
- **5 firms** were separately caught concealing a ban from another buyer in
  their own affidavit.
- **9 of 22** Karnataka records are currently stayed by the High Court.
- A test against the researcher's own, separate CDSCO national NSQ-alert
  dataset found **94 matching firms** - too mixed a result (alerts rose
  overall, but both Zee and Nestor individually fell after their own ban)
  to call this evidence that bans change later behaviour either way.

## 3 recommendations

1. A shared national blacklist, covering every state and national
   procurement body - the US's SAM.gov is the model (see the full report).
2. One common eligibility clause, used by every state, that unambiguously
   reaches a Not-of-Standard-Quality finding by *any* other agency.
3. Align the bid-time affidavit and the mid-contract cancellation clause to
   the same ground (Not of Standard Quality) the eligibility clause itself
   uses - not the narrower "spurious or adulterated" they currently ask
   about.
